# EarthSynoptic Code of Conduct

EarthSynoptic is committed to maintaining a professional, respectful, scientifically responsible, and safe environment for everyone who participates in the project.

This Code of Conduct defines the minimum standards of behavior expected in EarthSynoptic community spaces and project interactions.

## Our Commitment

EarthSynoptic welcomes constructive participation from people with different backgrounds, disciplines, levels of experience, perspectives, and areas of expertise.

Participation should be free from harassment, intimidation, discrimination, personal hostility, and other conduct that interferes with professional collaboration.

Project discussions may involve technical disagreement, scientific uncertainty, competing interpretations, architectural trade-offs, and critical review. Disagreement is expected. Disrespectful conduct is not.

## Expected Conduct

Participants are expected to:

- communicate professionally and respectfully;
- critique ideas, evidence, methods, designs, and implementations rather than attacking individuals;
- distinguish fact, evidence, interpretation, uncertainty, and opinion where relevant;
- accept constructive technical and scientific review;
- acknowledge and correct material errors when identified;
- respect reasonable differences in expertise, experience, and perspective;
- avoid knowingly misleading other participants;
- respect privacy and confidentiality;
- follow applicable repository security and contribution policies;
- avoid disrupting project collaboration;
- and act in a manner consistent with a professional scientific and engineering environment.

## Unacceptable Conduct

Unacceptable conduct includes, but is not limited to:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, or stalking;
- discriminatory or demeaning treatment;
- personal insults or sustained personal attacks;
- sexualized language, imagery, attention, or conduct inappropriate to a professional project environment;
- deliberate humiliation or targeted hostility;
- trolling intended primarily to provoke or disrupt;
- repeated bad-faith disruption of technical or scientific discussion;
- publishing another person's private information without appropriate authorization;
- impersonation or deliberate misrepresentation of another participant;
- retaliation against a person for raising a good-faith concern;
- encouraging others to engage in prohibited conduct;
- and other behavior that a reasonable person would regard as seriously inappropriate in a professional collaborative environment.

Legitimate criticism, evidence-based disagreement, correction of inaccurate claims, and good-faith scientific or technical scrutiny are not themselves violations of this Code of Conduct.

## Scientific and Technical Integrity

Because EarthSynoptic is intended to work with geoscience, Earth-system, and natural-hazard information, community conduct also includes responsibilities related to scientific and technical integrity.

Participants must not knowingly:

- fabricate or falsify scientific evidence, citations, datasets, measurements, model outputs, or test results;
- misrepresent simulated or derived information as direct observation;
- conceal material uncertainty or limitations in order to create a misleading conclusion;
- present unverified information as authoritative when verification is required;
- deliberately alter provenance or attribution in a misleading manner;
- or use project communication channels to promote claims that are knowingly unsupported by the evidence presented.

Good-faith scientific error, incomplete knowledge, methodological disagreement, and uncertainty are not misconduct merely because a conclusion later proves incorrect.

What matters is whether participants act transparently, respond constructively to evidence, and correct material inaccuracies when established.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to EarthSynoptic project spaces and project-related interactions, including where applicable:

- GitHub issues;
- pull requests;
- code review;
- discussions;
- commits and commit messages;
- repository documentation;
- project communication channels;
- project-managed events or meetings;
- and communications made by a person while officially representing EarthSynoptic.

Conduct outside project spaces may be relevant when a participant is acting as an official representative of the project or when external conduct directly and materially affects the safety or functioning of EarthSynoptic community spaces.

This policy is not intended to regulate unrelated private activity or lawful differences of opinion outside the project's legitimate scope.

## Maintainer Responsibilities

The EarthSynoptic maintainer is responsible for interpreting and enforcing this Code of Conduct during the project's current foundation stage.

The maintainer may take reasonable and proportionate action in response to conduct that violates this policy.

Depending on the circumstances, actions may include:

- requesting that conduct stop or be corrected;
- issuing a private or public warning where appropriate;
- editing, hiding, locking, or removing project content where platform permissions allow;
- closing or restricting an issue, pull request, discussion, or other interaction;
- temporarily limiting participation;
- permanently restricting participation;
- or escalating conduct to GitHub or another responsible service provider when appropriate.

Enforcement decisions should consider severity, persistence, context, impact, prior conduct, good-faith correction, and the safety of affected participants.

## Reporting Conduct Concerns

Do not use a public issue, pull request, discussion, commit, or other public project channel to disclose sensitive details about harassment, threats, personal information, or other conduct concerns that reasonably require confidentiality.

During the current foundation stage, a conduct concern may be reported privately to the repository maintainer using a private contact method published on the repository owner's GitHub profile.

A report should include, where reasonably available:

- a concise description of the conduct;
- the relevant project location or communication channel;
- links, screenshots, timestamps, or other supporting information;
- the identities or usernames involved, if known;
- any immediate safety or privacy concern;
- and whether the reporter has a specific confidentiality concern.

Do not include unnecessary personal data, credentials, confidential third-party information, or unrelated sensitive material.

Security vulnerabilities must continue to be reported through the process defined in the repository's [Security Policy](SECURITY.md), not through the conduct-reporting process unless the matter also involves a conduct concern.

## Confidentiality and Privacy

Conduct reports should be handled with appropriate discretion.

Information should be shared only to the extent reasonably necessary to understand the report, protect participants, obtain relevant advice, comply with legal obligations, or implement an appropriate response.

Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed where disclosure is required by law, necessary to address an immediate safety concern, or necessary to operate the applicable platform or enforcement process.

Retaliation against a person for making a good-faith report or participating in a good-faith review is prohibited.

## Fair Review

A reported person should not be presumed to have violated this Code of Conduct solely because a report has been submitted.

Where circumstances permit, enforcement should consider available evidence, relevant context, the seriousness of the alleged conduct, and any response provided by the person concerned.

Knowingly false or malicious reports may themselves constitute misconduct.

A report that cannot be substantiated does not, by itself, establish that the report was knowingly false or malicious.

## Proportional Response

EarthSynoptic does not use an automatic penalty schedule.

Responses should be proportionate to the conduct and may take into account:

- severity;
- actual or reasonably foreseeable harm;
- whether the conduct was deliberate;
- whether it was repeated;
- whether a prior warning was ignored;
- whether corrective action was taken;
- risk to other participants;
- and the need to preserve a safe and functional project environment.

Serious conduct may justify immediate restriction without a prior warning.

## Conflicts of Interest and Current Governance Limitation

EarthSynoptic is currently maintained under a foundation-stage governance model and does not yet have an independent Code of Conduct committee or formal appeals panel.

If a report concerns the sole maintainer or presents another material conflict of interest, the project cannot currently claim an independent internal review process.

This limitation is stated explicitly rather than implying governance mechanisms that do not yet exist.

As project governance develops, an independent escalation or review mechanism may be established and documented.

## Platform Rules and Law

This Code of Conduct supplements, and does not replace, applicable GitHub policies, platform terms, repository security rules, or applicable law.

Conduct that may violate GitHub policies may also be reported to GitHub through the appropriate platform mechanism.

Nothing in this policy authorizes access, investigation, disclosure, surveillance, or enforcement activity that would otherwise be unlawful or unauthorized.

## Relationship to Other Project Policies

This Code of Conduct should be read together with the repository's:

- [Contributing Guidelines](CONTRIBUTING.md);
- [Security Policy](SECURITY.md);
- and applicable repository rules and licensing terms.

Where another project policy establishes a more specific process for a particular issue, that specific process should normally be followed.

## Policy Evolution

This Code of Conduct reflects EarthSynoptic's current foundation-stage governance.

It will be reviewed as the project introduces additional maintainers, formal governance, community infrastructure, operational services, or other responsibilities that require a more developed moderation and appeals process.
