# EarthSynoptic Attribution

This document defines the institutional baseline for identifying, preserving, presenting, reviewing, and maintaining attribution associated with external scientific, geospatial, technical, and other third-party materials used by EarthSynoptic.

It complements [DATA_SOURCES.md](DATA_SOURCES.md), which governs scientific source authority and provenance, and [DATA_LICENSES.md](DATA_LICENSES.md), which governs source-specific rights, restrictions, and obligations.

This document is governance documentation and is not legal advice.

## Purpose

EarthSynoptic is intended to use scientific and geospatial information from multiple external sources.

Some sources may require attribution, acknowledgement, citation, copyright identification, license references, modification statements, notices, hyperlinks, or other forms of credit.

EarthSynoptic must preserve such requirements accurately when they apply.

Attribution must be based on verified evidence rather than assumption, convention, visual appearance, technical access, or informal copying from another product.

## Current Registry Status

EarthSynoptic is presently in the foundation and research stage.

There are currently no registered external data-source attribution entries in this document.

No provider-specific attribution text, credit line, copyright statement, license notice, acknowledgement, hyperlink requirement, citation format, logo requirement, trademark permission, or endorsement relationship should be inferred merely because a source, institution, dataset, API, publication, map, service, or product is discussed elsewhere.

This empty baseline does not waive or replace any attribution requirement that may later be established through authoritative source-specific evidence.

## Attribution, Provenance, Citation, and Licensing

Attribution, scientific provenance, scholarly citation, licensing, and legal notice preservation are related but distinct concepts.

Scientific provenance identifies where data or information came from and how it was transformed.

Scholarly citation identifies the scientific or academic work being referenced according to appropriate citation practice.

License attribution satisfies credit or identification requirements established by a license, terms of use, contract, waiver, rights statement, or other governing authority.

A legally required notice may need to be preserved separately and more exactly than an ordinary attribution statement.

Providing attribution does not itself establish permission to use material.

Likewise, permission to use material does not eliminate an attribution requirement when one applies.

## Attribution Authority

A required attribution should be based on the strongest available authoritative evidence.

Relevant first-party evidence may include:

- dataset-specific license terms;
- provider terms of use;
- official attribution instructions;
- official metadata;
- official copyright statements;
- official API documentation;
- official data policies;
- official citation guidance;
- contractual requirements;
- legally applicable notices;
- or other authoritative provider instructions.

Secondary summaries may assist discovery but should not replace authoritative source-specific requirements when those requirements are available.

EarthSynoptic must not invent mandatory attribution wording where no mandatory wording has been established.

## Required Attribution Record

A future registered attribution entry should document, where applicable:

- stable EarthSynoptic source identifier corresponding to DATA_SOURCES.md;
- corresponding data-license record or review status;
- provider or rights holder;
- dataset, product, service, collection, publication, or resource name;
- attribution requirement status;
- attribution category;
- exact mandatory wording where authoritative terms prescribe exact wording;
- provider name required for display;
- copyright statement where required;
- license name or identifier where required;
- acknowledgement text where required;
- citation form where required;
- hyperlink or reference requirement where applicable;
- modification or transformation statement where required;
- placement requirement;
- visibility requirement;
- persistence requirement;
- map-specific presentation requirement where applicable;
- export or download requirement where applicable;
- API or machine-readable requirement where applicable;
- derived-product requirement;
- composite-product requirement;
- logo, seal, mark, or branding restrictions where applicable;
- prohibition against implying endorsement where applicable;
- authoritative evidence location;
- evidence review date;
- applicable version, revision, or terms date;
- unresolved ambiguity;
- and review notes.

Unknown requirements must remain identified as unknown or unresolved rather than being replaced with invented attribution.

## Attribution Categories

Attribution requirements may arise from different authorities and should remain distinguishable.

Relevant categories may include:

- license-required attribution;
- terms-of-use attribution;
- copyright identification;
- scientific acknowledgement;
- scholarly citation;
- provider-required source credit;
- modification disclosure;
- legal notice preservation;
- contractual attribution;
- trademark or branding restriction;
- and other source-specific requirements.

A single source may require more than one category at the same time.

## Exact Required Wording

Where authoritative terms prescribe exact attribution, acknowledgement, copyright, license, or notice wording, EarthSynoptic should preserve that wording accurately.

Required text must not be shortened, paraphrased, translated, reformatted, or combined in a way that materially changes an obligation unless the governing terms permit that treatment.

Where no exact wording is prescribed, EarthSynoptic should not falsely describe a project-authored credit line as provider-mandated text.

Verbatim legal notices should remain distinguishable from project-generated explanatory text.

## Presentation and Placement

Attribution should be presented in a manner consistent with the applicable source-specific requirement.

Depending on the source and product, attribution may need to appear in:

- map interfaces;
- layer-information panels;
- data-detail views;
- exported images;
- exported maps;
- downloadable files;
- generated reports;
- scientific figures;
- application documentation;
- API metadata;
- machine-readable manifests;
- offline packages;
- or other distribution surfaces.

Placement must not be selected solely for visual convenience when authoritative terms prescribe another treatment.

## Visibility and Accessibility

Required attribution should not be deliberately obscured, truncated, hidden behind inaccessible interactions, rendered unreadably, or removed through responsive layout behavior.

Where attribution is displayed in an interactive user interface, EarthSynoptic should consider readability, keyboard access, assistive-technology compatibility, responsive behavior, and equivalent access on supported platforms.

Accessibility treatment must not alter mandatory wording or remove legally or scientifically relevant information.

## Maps and Geospatial Visualizations

Map-based products may combine data, imagery, basemaps, boundaries, labels, derived layers, and other resources from multiple sources.

Each layer may carry independent attribution or licensing requirements.

A single visible map must not imply that all displayed material originates from one provider.

Attribution architecture for maps should therefore support source-specific and layer-specific requirements where necessary.

Turning a source into a rendered map layer does not erase its attribution obligations.

## Derived and Transformed Products

Transformation does not automatically eliminate attribution.

Derived outputs may include:

- reprojected data;
- resampled data;
- aggregated datasets;
- classifications;
- interpolations;
- statistical products;
- rendered layers;
- derived indicators;
- model inputs;
- model outputs;
- analytical reports;
- or composite visualizations.

Where attribution obligations continue into derived products, EarthSynoptic should preserve the relationship between the derived output and the required source credit.

A derived product must not be presented as wholly original EarthSynoptic data when that characterization would conceal material external-source provenance.

## Modification Disclosure

Some licenses or provider terms may require changes, transformations, adaptations, or modifications to be indicated.

Where such a requirement applies, EarthSynoptic should record and present an appropriate modification statement.

A modification disclosure must distinguish the original provider's material from EarthSynoptic processing or interpretation.

EarthSynoptic must not attribute its own transformations, modelling choices, analytical conclusions, or errors to the original provider.

## Composite and Multi-Source Products

Composite products may incorporate multiple independently governed sources.

Attribution for one source must not displace attribution required for another source.

Where multiple attributions can lawfully and accurately be consolidated, the consolidated presentation should remain traceable to the underlying source records.

The shortest attribution presentation is not automatically the correct attribution presentation.

Material source credits must not be omitted merely because a composite product contains many sources.

## Machine-Readable and API Outputs

Where attribution must accompany machine-readable data, API output, metadata, downloaded files, or service responses, EarthSynoptic should preserve the required information in a technically appropriate form.

A graphical interface attribution alone may be insufficient for material that is redistributed independently through another interface.

Machine-readable attribution should remain linked to the applicable source identifier where feasible.

## Exports, Downloads, and Offline Use

Attribution requirements may persist after data leaves an interactive EarthSynoptic interface.

Where required, exported maps, images, reports, datasets, offline packages, or downloadable products should carry sufficient attribution information to remain compliant and scientifically traceable outside the application.

EarthSynoptic must not rely solely on a web-page footer when the distributed artefact itself must preserve attribution.

## Notice Preservation

Attribution must not be used as a substitute for a legally required notice when the applicable terms require preservation or reproduction of the notice itself.

Required notices, acknowledgements, copyright statements, or license text should be handled according to their actual governing conditions.

If a verified obligation later requires repository-level or distribution-level NOTICE or third-party-notice material, that requirement should be implemented from the authoritative obligation rather than from an invented placeholder.

The current external-data attribution registry contains no registered notice-preservation entry.

## Provider Identity, Branding, and Endorsement

Factual source attribution does not imply provider endorsement of EarthSynoptic.

Permission to use data does not automatically authorize use of provider logos, seals, trademarks, service marks, visual identity, or promotional branding.

EarthSynoptic must not imply sponsorship, certification, partnership, operational responsibility, or endorsement unless such a relationship actually exists and is documented.

Provider branding restrictions should be tracked separately where they materially affect attribution presentation.

## Attribution and Scientific Responsibility

Attribution does not transfer responsibility for EarthSynoptic processing or interpretation to the source provider.

EarthSynoptic should clearly distinguish:

- provider-supplied observations;
- provider-supplied derived products;
- EarthSynoptic transformations;
- EarthSynoptic statistical analysis;
- EarthSynoptic model output;
- EarthSynoptic visualization;
- and EarthSynoptic interpretation.

Source providers must not be represented as responsible for EarthSynoptic-derived conclusions unless authoritative evidence establishes that relationship.

## Attribution Conflicts and Ambiguity

Attribution requirements may be unclear, incomplete, inconsistent, or apparently conflicting across provider documents.

EarthSynoptic must not silently select the least burdensome interpretation merely because it produces a cleaner interface.

Where requirements remain unresolved, the ambiguity should be documented and the affected use should remain subject to further review.

Unknown attribution status must not be treated as evidence that no attribution is required.

## Terms Changes and Versioning

Attribution requirements can change when licenses, provider terms, metadata, policies, products, or service conditions change.

Source-specific attribution records should therefore preserve an evidence review date and, where possible, an applicable version, revision, release, or terms date.

A later provider-policy change must not silently rewrite the attribution conditions associated with an earlier reproducible workflow.

Material changes should trigger review of affected EarthSynoptic products and presentation surfaces.

## Attribution Automation

Future EarthSynoptic interfaces may generate attribution dynamically from registered source and license metadata.

Automated attribution must remain traceable to authoritative source records rather than relying on undocumented hard-coded strings.

Automation should not suppress an attribution merely because the relevant source is visually hidden, aggregated, transformed, or used indirectly when the governing requirement still applies.

Failure of an attribution-rendering component must not silently convert a known required attribution into no attribution.

## Registry Change Control

Adding or modifying an attribution record is a governance action.

A proposed attribution record should identify the authoritative evidence supporting the requirement and its relationship to the applicable source and license records.

Material updates should document:

- what attribution requirement changed;
- why it changed;
- which evidence supports the change;
- which EarthSynoptic products or interfaces are affected;
- whether historical artefacts are affected;
- and whether downstream redistribution requires review.

Historical attribution records should remain traceable where necessary for reproducibility or compliance.

## Current Limitations

EarthSynoptic does not currently maintain a complete attribution registry, automated attribution engine, production map-attribution compositor, export-attribution system, or automated terms-monitoring service.

The repository currently contains no registered external data-source attribution entries.

The absence of an attribution entry must not be interpreted as evidence that attribution is unnecessary.

Source-specific attribution requirements must be established from authoritative evidence before EarthSynoptic represents a source's attribution obligations as known.
